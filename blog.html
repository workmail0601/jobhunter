How to Write an ATS-Friendly CV for STEM & NGO Jobs in Kenya (2026 Guide)

More than 78% of large employers in Kenya now use an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) to filter CVs before a human ever reads them. If yours isn't formatted correctly, the role you've been training for since university may be rejected in under six seconds — not by a recruiter, but by a script.

This guide is written specifically for STEM and NGO professionals in Kenya. It tells you exactly what the ATS looks for, which keywords work for each sector, and the formatting choices that quietly kill applications.

What an ATS actually does

An ATS parses your CV into a structured database — name, contact info, work history, education, skills — then matches that profile against the job description. Each applicant gets a relevance score. Recruiters typically only see the top 20–25%.

The two things that crash that score for Kenyan applicants are fancy formatting (text boxes, two-column layouts, headers/footers) and missing keywords (using "data analyst" when the job advert says "monitoring and evaluation officer").

The 9 rules of an ATS-friendly Kenyan CV

  1. Use a single-column layout. Two-column "creative" templates from Canva almost never parse correctly.
  2. Save as .docx or PDF (text-based), never as an image. If you can't highlight the text with your cursor, the ATS can't read it.
  3. Standard section headings only: Professional Summary, Work Experience, Education, Skills, Certifications, Referees.
  4. Avoid headers and footers for contact info — many ATS parsers ignore them. Put your phone and email in the body of the CV.
  5. Spell out acronyms once. Write "Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E)" the first time so it matches both keywords.
  6. Match the job advert's exact phrasing. If they say "logframe", don't write "log-frame" or "logical framework".
  7. Use reverse-chronological order. Functional CVs confuse the parser.
  8. Quantify every bullet. "Trained 240 community health volunteers across 6 sub-counties" beats "trained many CHVs".
  9. Keep it to 2 pages unless you have 15+ years' experience.

The Kenya STEM keyword list (steal this)

If your CV is missing 3–4 of these, you're probably being filtered out for roles you're qualified for:

  • Engineering: AutoCAD, Revit, ArcGIS, BOQ, NCA registration, EBK, IET-Kenya, FIDIC, ISO 9001, HSE, SCADA.
  • Software & data: Python, SQL, Power BI, Tableau, AWS, Git, REST API, Agile, Scrum, CI/CD, KQL.
  • Public health: KEMSA, DHIS2, KHIS, Ministry of Health, county health system, USAID, PEPFAR, HIV/TB, malaria, RMNCAH, IPC.
  • Science & research: KEPHIS, NACOSTI permit, GCP, GLP, IRB approval, peer-reviewed, laboratory quality management.

The Kenya NGO keyword list

  • Logframe, theory of change, results framework, indicators, baseline, endline, MEAL, MEL, M&E.
  • Donor reporting: USAID, FCDO, GIZ, Sida, EU, UN agencies, BMZ, KOICA, JICA.
  • Compliance: 2 CFR 200, safeguarding, PSEA, anti-fraud, due diligence, sub-grant.
  • Programme: gender mainstreaming, GBV, livelihoods, WASH, food security, advocacy, capacity building.

The "summary paragraph" that wins interviews

Replace the generic "hardworking professional seeking growth" line with a 3-sentence summary that names your sector, years of experience, and 2–3 measurable wins. Example:

"Monitoring & Evaluation Officer with 6 years' experience designing logframes and DHIS2 dashboards for USAID and FCDO-funded health projects across 12 Kenyan counties. Led data-quality assessments that increased indicator reporting accuracy from 71% to 96% in 2025. Strong skills in Power BI, KoboToolbox, and donor reporting."

Final check: run your CV through a free ATS test

Before you apply, paste your CV and the job advert into our free ATS score checker. If you score below 70%, fix the missing keywords before pressing submit.

NGO Salary Guide Kenya 2026: What Development Sector Roles Really Pay

NGO salaries in Kenya are one of the most poorly understood corners of the job market. Donors rarely publish pay scales, glassdoor data is thin, and most candidates accept the first number offered. This guide pulls together verified ranges from 50+ international and local organisations operating in Kenya in 2025.

How Kenyan NGO pay is structured

Most INGOs use a national-staff grade system (e.g. NS-1 to NS-12). Local NGOs more commonly tie pay to project budgets, which means two officers doing identical work can earn very different salaries depending on the funder. As a rule of thumb:

  • UN agencies & large INGOs (UNDP, UNICEF, WFP, IFRC, Save the Children, Plan International) — top of the market, often with hardship and dependency allowances.
  • Mid-sized INGOs (Amref, PATH, FHI 360, CARE, World Vision, Oxfam) — 70–85% of UN rates.
  • Strong local NGOs (KEMRI Wellcome, AMPATH, LVCT, KELIN) — competitive for technical roles, lower for entry-level.
  • Project-funded local NGOs — highly variable; always ask about contract length and gratuity.

What pushes you to the top of the range

  1. Donor-specific compliance experience — USAID 2 CFR 200, FCDO accountability, EU PRAG. Each one adds roughly KES 30,000–60,000 to your offer.
  2. A second language — French and Arabic open Regional Office roles.
  3. Quantitative M&E — Power BI, R, Stata, and DHIS2 are paid more than generic "data analysis".
  4. Field deployment willingness — Turkana, Garissa, Mandera, Wajir attract real premiums.

Negotiation tactics that work in Kenya

Most NGOs will not negotiate base salary above the grade ceiling, but they almost always have room on: housing allowance, education allowance for dependants, R&R cycles, gratuity, and signing of a longer contract. Ask for those before asking for more base.

The Highest-Paying Engineering Jobs in Kenya & How to Land Them

Kenya's engineering market in 2026 is shaped by three forces: the Lamu–Lokichar oil corridor, a renewable-energy boom (geothermal, wind, solar), and rapid expansion of software and data infrastructure tied to the East Africa fintech wave. These are the highest-paying tracks today and the specific steps to break into each.

1. Petroleum & oil and gas engineering — KES 350k–1.8M

Operators (Tullow, TotalEnergies), service companies (Schlumberger, Halliburton, Baker Hughes) and EPC contractors continue to pay well above local market. Entry is competitive — most hires come through graduate programmes or via an MSc from Strathmore/JKUAT plus a year on rigs in Uganda or Mozambique.

How to break in: EBK registration (graduate level minimum), HSE Level 3 certification, and exposure to ASTM/API standards. A two-week IADC well-control short course makes a measurable difference on CVs.

2. Renewables & power engineering — KES 250k–950k

KenGen, Geothermal Development Company, Lake Turkana Wind Power, and a growing list of solar IPPs (e.g. M-KOPA Solar, d.light, Sun King) are hiring electrical, mechanical, and civil engineers fast. Battery storage and mini-grid project engineers are the most under-supplied roles.

How to break in: ERC technician/engineer licence, exposure to PVsyst or HOMER, and a portfolio of at least one designed system — even a residential rooftop.

3. Software engineering — KES 180k–1.2M

Local fintechs (M-Pesa, Cellulant, Tala, Branch), global remote employers (Andela, Toptal, Turing), and Nairobi-based scaleups now pay senior engineers above what most NGO country directors earn. Backend (Go, Java, Node) and platform/SRE roles command the highest rates.

How to break in: Public GitHub portfolio, contributions to an open-source project, and a clean LinkedIn that lists exact stack. Many global remote employers screen on GitHub before they look at your CV.

4. Civil & structural engineering — KES 120k–700k

Steady demand from the Nairobi expressway pipeline, county roads programmes, and real-estate developers. Highest pay sits with international contractors on KeNHA-funded mega projects.

How to break in: NCA registration, AutoCAD Civil 3D, BOQ preparation under FIDIC contracts, and at least one site-supervisor stint before you apply for design roles.

5. Data engineering — KES 200k–1.1M

The fastest-growing pay band in 2026. Banks (KCB, Equity, NCBA), telcos, and Big-4 advisory teams are competing for engineers who can build data pipelines, not just dashboards.

How to break in: SQL fluency, one cloud platform certification (AWS or Azure), dbt + Airflow exposure, and a project that shows you've shipped data into a production system.

The single biggest mistake

Most engineers in Kenya apply with a generic CV listing "AutoCAD, MS Office, teamwork". Recruiters at the high-paying end want to see specific software versions, specific standards, and specific deliverables. Get yours tightened before applying for a senior role.

15 NGO Interview Questions Kenyan Recruiters Actually Ask (With Answers)

Most NGO interviews in Kenya are competency-based. That means recruiters score your answers against a rubric — they're not looking for charm, they're looking for evidence. The candidates who do best know the question categories in advance and structure every answer using the STAR framework (Situation, Task, Action, Result).

Category 1: Programme experience

  1. "Tell me about a project you delivered against a tight donor deadline." — Focus on how you prioritised, who you negotiated with, and what the funder reported.
  2. "Describe a time the project scope changed mid-cycle." — Show that you protected the budget and managed donor communication, not just that you adapted.
  3. "How do you balance community needs with donor priorities?" — Mention community-led indicators added during inception; never criticise the donor.

Category 2: M&E and reporting

  1. "Walk me through a logframe you've designed." — Bring a printed copy. Name the outcome, two indicators, and the data source.
  2. "How do you ensure data quality from field teams?" — Spot checks, DQAs, KoboToolbox skip logic, regular CHV refresher trainings.
  3. "Tell me about a time you presented findings that the leadership didn't want to hear." — Honesty + diplomacy wins this one.

Category 3: Safeguarding & ethics

  1. "How would you handle a safeguarding allegation against a senior colleague?" — Follow the policy, report up, do not investigate yourself, do not warn the alleged perpetrator.
  2. "Have you ever observed a conflict of interest in a procurement?" — Show you knew the policy and used the whistleblowing channel.

Category 4: Leadership & teamwork

  1. "Describe leading a remote field team across counties."
  2. "Tell me about a difficult colleague and how you handled it."
  3. "When have you delegated a task you'd rather have done yourself?"

Category 5: Sector knowledge

  1. "What do you think is the biggest challenge facing the health/WASH/livelihoods sector in Kenya right now?" — Cite a real recent policy: BHCF rollout, devolution funding gaps, climate finance access.
  2. "Which donors do you have direct experience with?" — Name the donor, the compliance regime, and the reporting cycle.
  3. "How would you mainstream gender into a programme that's not gender-focused?"
  4. "Where do you see yourself in 3 years?" — Tie your answer to a technical track within the organisation, not a generic "country director" answer.

The STAR rule, briefly

For every competency question, spend ~15 seconds on Situation, 15 on Task, 60 on Action, 30 on Result. The Result must include a number. "We improved reporting" loses; "We improved on-time reporting from 64% to 91% across 7 implementing partners in one quarter" wins.

Remote STEM & NGO Jobs in Kenya: A Realistic 2026 Roadmap

Remote work changed Nairobi's job market in 2021. By 2026 it has matured into a two-tier system: a handful of high-paying global remote tracks, and a much larger pool of "hybrid" roles that still require Nairobi presence. Knowing which is which saves a lot of wasted applications.

Roles that are genuinely remote-friendly

  • Software engineers, especially backend and DevOps.
  • Data analysts and BI developers serving global teams.
  • Technical writers and developer-relations professionals.
  • Grant writers and proposal consultants for international NGOs.
  • M&E consultants on short-term assignments.
  • UX designers and product managers for SaaS companies.

Roles that are usually NOT remote, despite the listing

  • Programme officers — donors require field presence for verification.
  • Field engineers — site visits, by definition, are not remote.
  • Public-health roles tied to county systems or clinical sites.
  • Most finance roles — Kenyan tax filings require local KRA access.

How USD-paying remote roles actually work

Global employers (Toptal, Andela, Turing, GitLab, Automattic, Deel-payrolled startups) usually pay $2,000–$8,000/month depending on seniority. They engage Kenyan talent through one of three structures:

  1. Employer of Record (Deel, Remote.com, Oyster) — you become an employee of the EOR, get a PAYE-compliant payslip, and have NHIF and NSSF deducted.
  2. Contractor — you invoice monthly. You must register as a sole proprietor or limited company and file your own taxes.
  3. Direct payroll on a Kenyan entity — only the largest companies (Microsoft, Google, IBM) have this.

What recruiters look for in a remote candidate

  • A working webcam and a quiet, well-lit room — judged in the first interview.
  • Written communication that is short, structured, and timezone-aware.
  • A LinkedIn that lists "Open to remote, EAT (UTC+3)" explicitly.
  • Async work samples on GitHub, Notion, or a portfolio site.

The Perfect NGO Cover Letter: A Kenyan Template That Gets Replies

Most NGO cover letters from Kenyan applicants fail for the same three reasons: they're too long, they restate the CV, and they don't speak the donor's language. A good cover letter is 250–320 words, has a sharp opening, and shows familiarity with the organisation's actual programmes.

The 4-paragraph structure

  1. Hook (40 words). Name the role, name one current programme of the organisation, and tie one of your wins to it.
  2. Why you fit (80 words). Three bullet-worthy achievements in narrative form. Include numbers and at least one donor name.
  3. Why this organisation (80 words). Show you've read the latest annual report or country strategy. Name a specific initiative.
  4. Close (50 words). Confirm availability, salary expectation (only if asked), and one polite call to action.

Copy-paste template (adapt every line)

Dear [Hiring Manager],

I'm writing in response to the [Job Title] vacancy advertised on StemNGO Jobs Kenya. Your current [Programme Name] in [County] has been a reference point for our team at [Current Employer], and I'd like to bring my experience in [Skill Area] to support its next phase.

Over the past [X] years I have: led the design of a [donor]-funded logframe that exceeded its outcome targets by [%]; managed a [KES amount] grant across [#] counties with a clean audit; and built a DHIS2 dashboard that reduced quarterly reporting time from [X] to [Y] days.

What draws me to [Organisation] specifically is your recent [Initiative / Report / Partnership]. The way your team has integrated [theme] into county-level service delivery aligns with the methodology I helped pilot at [Previous Project]. I would welcome the opportunity to bring this experience into a larger programme.

I am available to start within [notice period], and would be happy to discuss the role at your convenience. Thank you for considering my application.

Sincerely,
[Your name]

Three phrases to delete

  • "I am writing to express my interest" — replace with the hook.
  • "I am a fast learner and hardworking individual" — meaningless without evidence.
  • "Please find attached my CV for your kind consideration" — the recruiter can see the attachment.

One more thing

Address the letter to a named person whenever possible. A two-minute LinkedIn search to find the HR lead or programme manager raises reply rates measurably. If you cannot find a name, use "Dear Hiring Manager" — never "To Whom It May Concern".
